# Quantum progress under flagged dephasing

[Science guide](README.md) · [Repository](../README.md)

The lower bounds use the [common model](model.md). This page states the external quantum input and checks its application to stationary initialization. The amplitude variable $`u_t`$ is distinct from the renewal age $`A_t`$.

### The imported progress statement

[ARS Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00506v2) permits exogenous correlated dephasing flags revealed after each query. On the star with center $`0^N`$ and singleton leaves, its packing weight is $`W=N`$. Lemmas 6.7–6.8 provide a history-conditioned potential with

**Equation (10).**

```math
\Psi_0=0,\qquad 0\le\Psi_t\le3,
\qquad \mathbb E\Psi_T\ge\gamma_\epsilon,
```

Here

```math
\gamma_\epsilon=
\frac{\left(1-2\sqrt{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}\right)^2}{36}>0.
```

There is also a predictable $`u_t\in[0,1]`$ satisfying

**Equation (11).**

```math
\mathbb E[\Psi_t-\Psi_{t-1}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
\le\frac{12}{N}+\frac{12u_t}{\sqrt N}-2p_tu_t^2.
```

The filtration $`\mathcal F_{t-1}`$ contains the classical information available before query $`t`$, and $`p_t=\Pr(Z_t=1\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})`$. The initial-age enlargement used below is justified separately.

The potential is the expectation of $`\Pi_C+3\Pi_Q`$ on a normalized conditional purification, where $`\Pi_C,\Pi_Q`$ are orthogonal truth-and-hidden-record projectors. Thus its bound by 3 is pointwise. The terminal statement concerns overall worst-case correctness, not correctness conditional on each transcript. Only the center and leaves enter, so the search promise suffices. [ARS, Sections 5.1 and 6.1; Lemmas 6.7–6.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00506v2).

### Why the initial-age conditioning is valid

There is a small extension to spell out: the noise schedule and a revealed initial age are correlated. One cannot simply call the age an independent private coin or apply the terminal lemma separately at each age.

Sample $`(A_1,Z_1,\ldots,Z_T)`$ jointly, independently of the input. For each fixed value of $`A_1`$, the conditional schedule is still exogenous. Any algorithmic measurements can be purified and their outcomes retained as orthogonal workspace registers, with subsequent operations controlled on those registers. This is valid because future noise does not depend on those outcomes. Known inter-query isometries act on the algorithm's registers and commute with the truth-and-record projectors defining the potential. Their action preserves its unconditional value; conditioning on recorded measurement outcomes preserves it in expectation.

Equivalently, after this purification it is enough to condition the local analysis on the filtration generated by $`A_1`$ and the revealed noise flags, retaining all other algorithm branches coherently. At query $`t`$, conditional on this information, the fresh event has probability exactly $`h(A_t)`$. The normalized conditional-state inequalities underlying (11) therefore apply with

**Equation (12).**

```math
p_t=h(A_t).
```

The age label does not change the truth-space geometry or the norm bounds. Initially the truth state is the same star state for every age, so $`\Psi_0=0`$ for each initial-age branch.

For the terminal step, keep an additional orthogonal register containing the initial age in the **unconditional** common purification. On every input its initial distribution is the same. For input $`x`$, let the resulting final normalized purification be $`|\psi_x\rangle`$. Overall error at most $`\epsilon`$ implies that the final decision measurement distinguishes $`0^N`$ from every $`e_j`$. Consequently

**Equation (13).**

```math
|\langle\psi_{0^N}|\psi_{e_j}\rangle|
\le2\sqrt{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}.
```

The terminal projection argument is unaffected by carrying the orthogonal age register along with the other purification registers. Hence it gives (10) after averaging over age and flags. No assertion of bounded conditional error at every age is used.

This also justifies revealing $`A_1`$ to the algorithm for the lower bound: its age-controlled operations are still input-independent, and the unconditional terminal argument is unchanged. Such a lower bound applies in particular to the original hidden-age model.

### Mapping checks

The XOR oracle and query-index pinching are exactly the operations required in (11). The event flag arrives after pinching; the hidden pinching outcome remains inaccessible. Future flags are conditionally independent of algorithm outcomes and hidden index records once the noise history and initial age are fixed. Arbitrary workspace and adaptive controls are included in the conditional state. Padding halted algorithms preserves correctness and keeps the noise process stationary at every charged call index. No assumption of iid flags, finite gap variance, or finite mean stationary residual is used.


## Stationary hazard bound

One direct consequence will be sharp for the alternating-hazard construction. Put $`K=\sqrt N`$. Completing the square and using $`0\le u\le1`$ give, for $`p>0`$,

```math
\frac{12u}{K}-2pu^2
\le \min\left\{\frac{12}{K},\frac{18}{Np}\right\}
\le \frac{18}{N}\min\{K,p^{-1}\}.
```

The last bound also holds at $`p=0`$, with $`p^{-1}=+\infty`$. Define

```math
B_N=\mathbb E_\pi\min\{\sqrt N,h(A)^{-1}\}.
```

Since $`0\le h\le1`$, one has $`1\le B_N\le\sqrt N`$. Averaging the local inequality (11), using the stationary age law, and applying terminal progress (10) yields

**Equation (P).**

```math
\mathbb E\Psi_T\le\frac{30TB_N}{N},
\qquad
Q_\epsilon(N;L)\ge\frac{\gamma_\epsilon N}{30B_N}.
```

The bound includes arbitrary adaptive controls and protected memory, and remains valid with the initial age revealed. It is not an optimality formula for every renewal law. The [stopped proof](renewal-law.md#lower-bound-by-stopping-before-old-ages) is stronger for some tails because it treats rare old ages separately.
